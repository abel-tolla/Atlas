# Competitor Analysis

Most competitors are strong at AI scheduling inside Google Calendar or Outlook, but none of the pages reviewed describe Canvas support, human-owned priority, rolling routine cycles, or more than a few alerts per event. Pricing below is as listed on third-party review pages in October 2026 and differs between sources.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Motion](https://www.morgen.so/blog-posts/motion-review) | AI auto-schedules tasks and re-optimizes as things change | Two-way sync with Google and Outlook | Full task and project manager, at-risk flags, task chunking | Recurring tasks | [About $19 to $49 per month](https://us.fitgap.com/products/045904/motion), plan names vary by source | Closed task system, high price, works best when it controls the whole calendar |

## Where Atlas is different

| Atlas choice | Why it matters | Closest competitor behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canvas and student calendars alongside Google, Outlook and Apple | Students' deadlines live in Canvas, not in a work calendar. | Reviewed tools focus on Google and Outlook. |
| The user decides priority, and events can allow overlap | Working during a lecture is normal, and auto-ranking gets it wrong. | Motion and Reclaim rank and move things automatically. |
| Rolling-cycle routines that shift after a missed day | Training splits aren't weekday-based. | Reclaim habits and recurring tasks repeat on set days. |
| Plan from pasted instructions, with an editable review screen | Turns a deadline into blocks in one step. | Sunsama and Akiflow expect the user to plan by hand. |
| One-tap tracking that adjusts future estimates | Plans improve as the user tracks. | Reclaim says it learns from behavior, but no tracking step is described. |
| Up to 10 alerts per item | Big events need a countdown, not two alerts. | Not found in the pages reviewed. |
| AI only proposes, on a self-hosted model | Privacy and no per-request cost. | Motion's scheduler acts on the calendar automatically. |

## Strengths and weaknesses to plan around

- **Their strength: polish and automation.** Motion and Reclaim reschedule continuously. Atlas will feel slower and more manual by design, so the review screen must be fast.
- **Their strength: mature integrations.** Two-way sync is a real advantage. Atlas stays read-only, so the pitch is a clear single view, not full control.
- **Their weakness: price.** Sunsama, Akiflow and Motion have no free plan or a high monthly cost. Atlas can stay free for students.
- **Their weakness: Canvas and routines.** None of the pages reviewed describe either.
- **Our risk:** the self-hosted model may feel less capable. Keep AI output narrow and validated, and don't let the app depend on it.

## Limits of this report

Figures come from third-party review and pricing pages found by search, not from each vendor's own page, and some of those pages are written by competing products. I did not open each page in full, and sources disagree on current plan names and prices, so verify prices before quoting them. Fantastical, Notion Calendar and Canvas-specific sync tools were searched but returned too little reliable detail to include.
